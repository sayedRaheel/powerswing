0The problem

Simple
AI training makes power jump up and down every few seconds. At 100 MW that shakes the grid. Today sites waste power ("always-burn") to hide the dips. We asked: can we predict dips and only smooth when needed?
Deep
  • Grid ramp limit assumed: 10% of rated power per second (configurable).
  • Fix families (Microsoft/OpenAI/NVIDIA, arXiv 2508.14318): software fill, GPU power floor, storage.
  • Our angle: use a forecast to arm the fix only before a drop.

1Data

Simple
340 hours of real H100 power readings from a US national lab, training and inference. See Lesson 1.
Deep
  • NLR dataset, CC BY 4.0, 5 Hz training / 10 Hz inference, resampled to 5 Hz, GPU only.
  • Split by whole job (training) and by time (inference). Test = 9 jobs + 3 session tails.
  • src/load.py, data/README_data.md

2What is a "big drop"

Simple
Power falls by more than 30% of its recent peak within 1 second. We marked every one, then asked "will one start in the next 5 s?"
Deep
  • Fall > 0.3 x max(last 10 s) within 1 s, peak at least 1 kW, 5 s refractory.
  • Label y=1 if a drop onset falls in (t, t+5 s].
  • Rates per node-hour: SD 118, offline inference 113, finite 60, Llama 32, fixed-rate 24.
  • src/events.py

3Two tasks

Simple
A: guess the power 1, 5 and 10 seconds from now. B: raise an alarm before a big drop.
Deep
  • Task A metric: MAE in watts per node (also RMSE, MAPE).
  • Task B metrics: recall with at least 1 s warning, mean lead time, false alarms per node-hour, AP, F1.
  • Threshold picked on validation only (highest threshold with validation recall at least 0.85).

4Baselines (the simple rules to beat)

Simple
Persistence: "same as now". Moving average. Rhythm: "the pattern repeats, so copy the last cycle".
Deep
  • Rhythm uses a 120 s sliding autocorrelation to find the period (2 to 40 s), falls back to persistence when no rhythm.
  • Rhythm is the strong baseline because training is periodic.
  • src/baselines.py

5Models

Simple
LightGBM (a fast tree model on hand-made signals), a tiny neural net (TCN), and two big pre-trained forecasters used with no training (Chronos, TimesFM).
Deep
  • 56 causal features: lags, differences, rolling stats 1 to 60 s, time since last drop, rhythm period and phase, rhythm forecasts.
  • LightGBM: L1 regression on power change per horizon + drop classifier. src/features.py, src/models.py
  • TCN: 46k params, 51 s window, joint forecast + alarm, runs in a subprocess (macOS OpenMP clash). src/nets.py
  • Chronos-Bolt-Small, TimesFM 2.5: zero-shot on 2,000 sampled test points.

7Smoothing simulation

Simple
We replayed test traces and tried strategies. Always-burn wastes energy and still fails. Predictive switches smoothing on only before drops: fixes most violations for much less energy.
Deep
  • Strategies only add power (fill). Predictive arms a ramp-down limiter on alarm or forecast fall > 15% of peak; actuator latency 1 s, hold 6 s.
  • Node level: predictive removes 84% of violations for 2.6% energy; always-burn 7% for 6.8%; reactive 16%; always-on limiter 100% for 2.9%. criterion met
  • 100 MW synchronized site: always-burn $3.8M/yr, predictive $1.4M/yr at $80/MWh.
  • Random-offset site: worst fall 2.1 MW/s, never violates. src/simulate.py

8Honest limits

  • Smoothing does not need the ML: arming the same limiter with the simple rhythm alarm removes 83.3% of violations for 2.4% energy, vs ML 83.8% for 2.6% (found by Codex review, verified). The ML win is in forecast accuracy, not in the smoothing result.
  • Always-on limiter (no ML) matches predictive if the actuator is instant. Prediction pays off only with about 1 s or more actuator delay, or for pre-ramping.
  • About 12 independent test units; no confidence intervals yet.
  • 5 Hz misses sub-second swings; inference drops are benchmark pauses; actuator is simulated; one site.
  • Closest prior work: EasyRider (2604.15522, reactive hardware, same 10%/s target), 2609.22542 (same NLR data, battery smoothing), 2606.04869 (predictive control with perfect preview).
